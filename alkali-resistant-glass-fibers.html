    Alkali-Resistant Glass Fibers

    concretefiber-reinforcementglassmacrofiber
    Also known as: AR glass, AR glass macrofibers
    Created 2026-07-22 · Updated 2026-08-17T02:09:08Z

    Alkali-resistant glass fibers are composite reinforcement strands composed of a silica-based glass matrix modified with zirconium dioxide to withstand harsh chemical environments. The incorporation of zirconia facilitates the formation of a protective surface layer on individual filaments that shields the underlying material from corrosive agents [1].

    Alkali-Resistant Glass Fiber Performance

    Alkali-resistant glass fibers are engineered reinforcement strands designed to withstand the highly alkaline environment found within cementitious matrices [1]. The addition of zirconia facilitates the formation of a protective surface layer on individual filaments, which prevents alkali-induced degradation and allows the fibers to retain their mechanical strength within concrete structures [1].

    Research problem — Conventional silica or basalt fibers degrade rapidly in the highly alkaline environment of Portland cement, leading to a loss of tensile strength and jeopardizing durability [1]. Incorporating long monofilament alkali-resistant glass fibers is challenging because they are difficult to pack without generating air voids that reduce compressive strength at higher dosages [1].

    State of the art — Current state-of-the-art production relies on zirconium oxide substitution to form a protective surface layer, supplemented by alkali-resistant coatings or polymer resin impregnation to ensure durability in cementitious environments [1]. Modern practice utilizes hybrid systems combining modest dosages of AR glass macrofibers with polypropylene microfibers and high-range water reducers to enhance flexural capacity, toughness, and ductility without compromising workability or compressive strength [1].

    Findings from the reports

    • The study demonstrated that alkali-resistant (AR) glass macrofibers outperformed polypropylene (PP) and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) macrofibers in bridge-deck applications [1].
    • The research showed that AR glass fibers matched or exceeded the compressive strength of control mixes without diminishing it, with effectiveness increasing at higher dosages [1].
    • The evaluation revealed a dosage-dependent trend where low dosages (0.125%) increased 56-day compressive strength, while higher dosages caused modest reductions [1].
    • The analysis indicated that flexural performance improved at low dosages, with specific mixes showing significant increases in flexural strength and post-peak residual capacity [1].
    • The findings observed that AR glass fibers produced superior post-peak behavior, extending residual flexural capacity beyond one-hundred-fiftieth of the span length at 0.5% dosage [1].
    • The study reported that workability demands for AR glass fibers were comparable to PP fibers and lower than those for PVA fibers [1].
    • The results demonstrated a promising synergy between AR glass macrofibers and polypropylene (PP) microfibers, supporting their use in hybrid fiber-reinforced concrete mixes [1].

    Novelty & rigor — The research introduces a hybrid reinforcement strategy that synergizes chemically upgraded alkali-resistant glass macrofibers with low-dosage polypropylene microfibers to achieve superior flexural toughness and post-peak residual strength in concrete systems [1]. The methodology is distinguished by a specific two-step chemical treatment process that applies zirconium oxide and polymer coatings to silica or basalt glass fibers, ensuring durability against alkaline environments while maintaining effective bonding within the cementitious matrix [1]. Reproducibility is ensured through a standardized protocol involving Class F fly ash substitution, precise volume fractions for both fiber types, and strict adherence to standard curing and mechanical testing procedures at twenty-eight days [1].

    Implications — Designers should specify alkali-resistant glass macrofibers as the primary reinforcement in bridge-deck concrete to ensure adequate tensile capacity and post-peak flexural toughness without compromising compressive strength [1]. Specifications ought to mandate a hybrid fiber system combining polypropylene microfibers with alkali-resistant glass macrofibers to enhance crack resistance, reduce water demand, and achieve workable mixes that satisfy both service-level and ultimate-load requirements [1].

    Limitations — The performance benefits of alkali-resistant glass macrofibers are not universal and can be surpassed by alternative fiber types at specific dosage levels [1]. Practical constraints on workability and the tendency of long monofilament fibers to create air voids limit their effectiveness, particularly regarding compressive strength and interfacial bonding [1].
