    EverFE

    curlingfinite-elementmodelingrigid-pavementwarping
    Also known as: EverFE 2.24
    Created 2026-05-20 · Updated 2026-08-16T20:47:33Z

    EverFE is a specialized three-dimensional finite-element software package designed to simulate the structural response of jointed plain concrete pavement systems.

    EverFE Rigid Pavement Modeling & Longitudinal Cracking Analysis

    Rigid pavement systems are typically modeled using three-dimensional finite-element analysis to capture the complex structural interactions between concrete slabs, joints, and subgrade support. Specialized computational tools simulate these pavements by representing individual slabs and joint mechanisms, such as dowel bars or aggregate interlock, which govern shear transfer across slab boundaries [2]. Accurate prediction of cracking potential requires accounting for temperature-dependent material behavior and the combined effects of thermal gradients and traffic loads on pavement stresses [2].

    Research problem — There is a need to understand the underlying mechanisms of longitudinal cracking in widened jointed plain concrete pavements and how design choices like skewed joints influence stress distributions [2]. Existing finite element models lack the capability to simulate permanent strains such as shrinkage and creep when temperature differences are zero, leading to inaccurate predictions of early-age deformations that affect pavement performance [1].

    State of the art — The current state of the art for rigid concrete pavement analysis relies on three-dimensional finite-element modeling, with EverFE serving as the premier and most widely adopted tool in this domain [1] [2]. For longitudinal cracking assessment, engineers utilize advanced versions of EverFE that incorporate complex features such as skewed joints, aggregate interlock, dowel shear transfer, and nonlinear temperature effects to simulate realistic loading conditions [2]. While early-age analysis often collapses environmental influences into equivalent temperature differentials calibrated against field data, current practices for cracking specifically model negative temperature gradients to capture combined mechanical-thermal stresses that drive tensile stress concentrations [1] [2].

    Findings from the reports

    • EverFE 2.25 finite-element simulations demonstrated that widened jointed plain concrete pavement sections with skewed joints develop noticeably larger top-tensile stresses at transverse joints than comparable rectangular-joint slabs, indicating a greater risk of top-down longitudinal cracking [2].
    • Truck-load modeling using EverFE identified that four-axle configurations with axle loads positioned near slab edges produce very high top-to-bottom tensile stress ratios, explaining the observed initiation of cracks at transverse joints [2].
    • EverFE 2.25 simulations pinpointed critical locations where longitudinal cracks are most likely to initiate in widened slabs with skewed joints, confirming field observations of distress at those specific points [2].
    • The study concluded that EverFE 2.24 accurately predicts early-age slab curling and warping when its inputs are calibrated using an equivalent temperature difference that aggregates moisture, shrinkage, and other environmental effects [1].
    • With calibrated inputs, EverFE’s simulated corner-deflection and curvature closely tracked field measurements for both morning and afternoon paving cycles across diagonal and transverse directions [1].

    Novelty & rigor — The research introduces a novel methodology that applies EverFE finite-element modeling to simulate the combined effects of axle loads and temperature gradients on widened concrete slabs, specifically quantifying how skewed joints amplify longitudinal cracking potential compared to conventional rectangular layouts [2]. This approach leverages EverFE’s advanced capabilities for explicit representation of joint behaviors and nonlinear thermal effects, providing a more realistic simulation of slab mechanics than traditional tools [2].

    Across the reviewed reports, EverFE finite-element modeling establishes that specific geometric configurations and loading conditions significantly influence rigid pavement distress mechanisms, with widened slabs featuring skewed joints exhibiting markedly higher top-tensile stresses at transverse joints compared to rectangular-joint designs. While EverFE accurately predicts early-age slab deformation by calibrating environmental effects through equivalent temperature differences that align with MEPDG parameters, it simultaneously identifies critical stress ratios under heavy truck loads as the primary driver for longitudinal crack initiation at transverse joints. [1] [2]

    Implications — Practitioners must derive equivalent temperature inputs from field measurements to accurately model early-age slab behavior, as the software cannot directly capture moisture-driven or permanent deformations [1]. Design workflows should incorporate calibration steps that align environmental effects with effective curl and warp temperatures to ensure realistic predictions of deflection and curvature [1].

    Limitations — The model is restricted to capturing slab curvature driven by temperature differentials and cannot directly simulate moisture-induced or permanent deformations occurring without thermal changes [1]. Predictions rely on converting diverse environmental effects into an equivalent temperature through back-estimated regression, which introduces uncertainty by failing to quantify individual contributions from factors such as shrinkage or creep separately [1].
