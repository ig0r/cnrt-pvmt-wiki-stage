    Datum Temperature

    concretematuritytemperaturetesting
    Also known as: T₀
    Created 2026-05-20 · Updated 2026-08-17T11:52:33Z

    Datum temperature is a reference threshold that defines the lower limit of thermal activity below which concrete does not accumulate strength. This baseline value enables the conversion of a concrete’s thermal history into an equivalent age, thereby allowing for the prediction of strength development and construction scheduling [1]. The specific threshold varies with cement composition, as blends containing supplementary materials often exhibit higher datum temperatures due to the cessation of pozzolanic reactions at warmer levels compared to ordinary Portland cement [1]. Experimental determination involves curing specimens under controlled conditions and analyzing their strength evolution to derive kinetic parameters such as activation energy [1].

    Datum Temperature Of Blended Cement Concrete

    Datum temperature serves as a reference threshold in concrete maturity calculations, representing the point below which cementitious materials cease to gain strength [1]. This parameter establishes the baseline for converting thermal histories into equivalent ages via temperature-time factors, thereby enabling the prediction of strength development and scheduling of construction activities [1].

    Research problem — The widespread assumption of a datum temperature derived from ordinary Portland cement is unsuitable for blended cements, leading to erroneous strength predictions and potentially unsafe or inefficient pavement opening decisions [1]. There is a critical need to accurately record and apply the correct datum temperature in maturity-based strength prediction to ensure reliable degree-hour calculations under extreme ambient heat conditions [2].

    State of the art — Current standards recommend selecting a single datum temperature, whether measured or conventional, for all maturity calculations within a project to ensure consistent strength-time-temperature factor correlations and reliable pavement opening decisions [1].

    Findings from the reports

    • Ternary blends consistently showed higher datum temperatures than binary blends, and both fly ash and slag replacements increased the activation energy of the concrete [1].
    • Using the actual datum temperature shifted the strength-log(TTF) curve leftward and reduced the TTF required for traffic-opening strength, although the predicted opening time remained essentially unchanged when a consistent relationship was used [1].
    • Applying a TTF calibrated under normal-temperature conditions to concrete cured at colder temperatures led to an unsafe early opening prediction [1].

    Novelty & rigor — The research introduces a novel approach by empirically determining the specific datum temperature for binary and ternary blended cement mixes, challenging the traditional assumption of a uniform value for ordinary Portland cement [1]. This method ensures greater accuracy in maturity-based strength predictions by applying the measured datum temperature consistently during both the calibration of strength-maturity relationships and the prediction of pavement traffic opening times [1]. Reproducibility is achieved through a standardized experimental protocol involving mortar cubes cured at multiple temperatures, with subsequent analysis performed according to established maturity method standards to calculate datum temperature and activation energy [1].

    Implications — Designers should abandon the conventional datum temperature for blended cement concretes, as these mixes exhibit higher values that increase with greater fly ash or slag content [1]. Maturity-based time-to-opening calculations must employ measured datum temperatures consistently across strength correlations and time predictions to ensure reliable pavement opening times [1]. Specifications should require a single, clearly defined datum temperature for all maturity computations and prohibit the use of ordinary portland cement-derived correlations for slag or fly ash mixes to avoid premature traffic opening and insufficient pavement strength [1].

    Limitations — The applicability of the results is restricted to field conditions where actual temperature regimes align with the typical seasonal averages used in the study [1]. Predictions may be compromised if there is inconsistency between the datum temperature applied for strength correlation and that used for traffic-opening correction [1].
