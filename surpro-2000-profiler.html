    SurPRO 2000 Profiling Capabilities

    Rolling profilers utilize inclinometer-based sensors to capture longitudinal surface elevations at high spatial resolution, enabling detailed assessment of pavement smoothness and slab curvature [1]. This mobility allows for rapid data collection over entire test sections, making such devices suitable for monitoring early-age concrete pavements where quick turnaround is essential [1].

    Research problem — The reliance on rolling inclinometer technology prevents the SurPRO 2000 from capturing complete profile loops on newly placed concrete slabs, necessitating revised measurement patterns [1]. This hardware limitation hinders the acquisition of timely, high-quality surface profiles during critical early-age diurnal cycles when accurate data is most needed for pavement performance evaluation and quality control [1].

    State of the art — It acquires longitudinal profiles at one-inch spacing, which is far finer than the twelve-inch spacing of the Dipstick system and allows full-length Level B measurements within a single diurnal cycle [1]. Because its rolling nature precludes practical collection of closed-loop profiles, modified profiling patterns are employed to work around this limitation [1].

    Findings from the reports

    • The SurPRO 2000 profiler acquired longitudinal profiles at one-inch spacing, enabling full Level B coverage of test sections during both morning and afternoon diurnal cycles [1].
    • ANOVA testing revealed no statistically significant difference in smoothness indices between morning and afternoon measurements, with p-values exceeding 0.05 [1].
    • Early-age profiling with the SurPRO captured spatial variability that influenced pavement smoothness grading, as evidenced by IRI values reaching correction-grade levels at slab edges while mid-slab readings remained within the full-pay range [1].

    Novelty & rigor — The approach introduces a novel data acquisition strategy by utilizing a rolling inclinometer profiler to capture longitudinal pavement profiles at significantly higher spatial resolution and speed than legacy systems, enabling complete Level B profiling within single diurnal cycles [1]. Distinctive non-looping profile patterns were developed to accommodate the device’s rolling design, ensuring that data collection aligns with future traffic directions while maintaining reproducibility through strict adherence to specific instrument settings and environmental logging protocols [1].
