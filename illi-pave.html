    ILLI-PAVE

    conceptfinite-elementflexible-pavementsoftware
    Also known as: ILLI-PAVE 2000, ILLI-PAVE finite element program
    Created 2026-05-20 · Updated 2026-08-16T20:47:33Z

    ILLI-PAVE is an axisymmetric finite element program designed for the structural analysis of flexible pavements. The software utilizes nonlinear, stress-dependent resilient modulus material models to predict pavement response under traffic loading [1]. It incorporates specific soil modulus models that account for the softening behavior of fine-grained subgrade soils [1]. The finite element mesh is structured to capture stress concentrations and align with sensor locations such as falling weight deflectometer radial offsets [1].

    Key findings

    • Artificial neural networks accurately back-calculated pavement layer moduli and predicted critical responses from FWD deflection basins, achieving average absolute errors of approximately one percent for asphalt concrete modulus and subgrade resilient modulus [1].
    • The forward-calculation ANN model eliminated the need for a separate back-calculation step, significantly accelerating routine pavement evaluations while producing results that closely agreed with traditional statistical algorithms [1].

    ILLI-PAVE Finite Element Modeling

    Axisymmetric finite element analysis provides a robust framework for evaluating the structural response of flexible pavements under traffic loads. Accurate simulation requires incorporating nonlinear, stress-dependent resilient modulus models to capture material behavior and realistic soil modulus representations to account for subgrade softening [1]. Specialized finite element meshes are employed to resolve localized stress concentrations and align with specific sensor locations for precise response prediction [1].

    Research problem — Traditional back-calculation methods for extracting pavement layer moduli from Falling Weight Deflectometer data are slow, cumbersome, and rely on linear-elastic assumptions that fail to capture the stress-dependent nonlinear behavior of real geomaterials [1]. The manual selection of seed layer moduli and extensive pre- and post-processing required by these approaches makes routine network-level pavement evaluation impractical for department engineers [1].

    State of the art — ILLI-PAVE remains the premier axisymmetric finite-element tool for flexible pavement analysis, utilizing realistic stress-dependent material models and enhanced computational capabilities in its updated versions [1]. The current state of the art integrates these finite-element solutions with backpropagation artificial neural networks to instantly back-calculate layer moduli and predict critical stresses from deflection basins [1]. This combined finite-element and ANN approach is now the leading practice for nondestructive pavement assessment among Iowa DOT engineers and other agencies due to its speed and accuracy [1].

    Findings from the reports

    • ANN models reproduced ILLI-PAVE layer-modulus and critical response predictions with high fidelity, achieving average absolute errors below 1.5% overall [1].
    • The ANN-based back-calculation models successfully predicted pavement layer moduli values (except for the base/subbase layer in flexible pavements) with an overall AAE value of less than 1.5 percent [1].
    • ANN tools evaluated up to one hundred thousand FWD deflection profiles in under a second, operating approximately two million times faster than the original ILLI-PAVE finite-element solutions [1].
    • The forward-calculation ANN eliminated the separate back-calculation step, greatly accelerating routine pavement evaluations and producing results that agreed closely with traditional statistical algorithms and the FAA BAKFAA program [1].

    Novelty & rigor — The approach is novel for integrating a comprehensive database of stress-dependent nonlinear finite element simulations with artificial neural networks to bypass traditional iterative back-calculation procedures [1]. This fusion enables the direct prediction of layer moduli and critical responses from surface deflection data, achieving computational speeds millions of times faster than conventional finite element runs while maintaining robustness against measurement noise [1].

    Implications — Engineers can leverage the ILLI-PAVE finite element database to train neural networks that provide rapid, full-depth backcalculations and forward predictions with high accuracy [1]. Incorporating modest noise into training sets ensures model robustness, enabling reliable pavement condition assessments even when field deflection data contains measurement errors [1]. This approach allows for real-time evaluation of pavement sections at both project and network scales without requiring specialized finite element expertise or extensive processing time [1].

    Limitations — The model’s accuracy degrades significantly when applied to real-world field data or inputs containing measurement noise, unlike its performance on synthetic datasets [1]. Predictions for subgrade modulus exhibit greater scatter than those for asphalt concrete due to higher inherent variability in the underlying soil conditions [1].
