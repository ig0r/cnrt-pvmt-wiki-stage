    Iowa Pore Index

    aggregateconceptdurabilitypore-structuretesting
    Also known as: 3G IPI, IPI, Iowa Pore Index Phase II, Iowa Pore Index Test, Pore Pressure Index
    Created 2026-07-22 · Updated 2026-08-14T09:07:44Z

    The Iowa Pore Index is a rapid laboratory test that evaluates the pore structure of coarse aggregate by forcing water into dry material under constant pressure. The procedure quantifies macropores and micropores separately by recording the volume of water absorbed during an initial short interval to represent larger voids, followed by a longer interval to capture intrusion into finer capillary pores [1] [2] [3] [4]. These sequential measurements yield distinct primary and secondary indices that characterize the aggregate’s porosity distribution [1] [3] [4]. Because fine pores are closely linked to freeze-thaw durability, these values serve as indicators of an aggregate’s susceptibility to damage in cold climates [2] [3] [4].

    Fundamentals

    The Iowa Pore Index is a rapid laboratory method that evaluates the pore structure of coarse aggregates by forcing water into dry material under constant pressure. The test distinguishes between macropores and micropores by recording the volume of water absorbed during an initial short interval versus a subsequent longer period. Because fine capillary pores are closely linked to freeze-thaw durability, the secondary measurement serves as an indicator of susceptibility to damage in cold climates.
