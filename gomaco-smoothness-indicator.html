    GOMACO Smoothness Indicator (GSI)

    conceptconstructionequipmentprofilingreal-time-monitoring
    Also known as: GSI
    Created 2026-05-20 · Updated 2026-08-17T06:21:51Z

    The Gomaco Smoothness Indicator is a profiling instrument mounted on slipform paving equipment that continuously records pavement surface elevation across the width of the slab. It generates continuous longitudinal profile data along the length of the concrete overlay to evaluate surface smoothness and ride quality [3].

    GSI Real-Time Roughness Detection

    The GSI is a profiling instrument mounted on slipform paving equipment that continuously records pavement surface elevation across the slab width [3]. By capturing this continuous profile data along the length of the overlay, the system provides immediate feedback to assess real-time roughness and ride quality during construction [3].

    Research problem — The GSI system introduces unwanted short-wavelength noise and sensor vibration artifacts that artificially inflate real-time roughness readings, preventing accurate assessment of pavement smoothness during construction [1] [4]. These measurement distortions obscure true surface quality, hindering the ability to guide immediate corrective actions or verify compliance with agency acceptance criteria while paving operations are ongoing [3] [4].

    State of the art — The GOMACO Smoothness Indicator (GSI) is an established on-site profiling tool used to capture pavement profiles in plastic concrete and flag localized roughness issues such as dowel-basket ripple [1]. While the device cannot directly predict final smoothness incentive scores due to differences from post-construction inertial profilers, it is accepted as a real-time quality-control device with defined sensor placement practices and manufacturer guidelines [1].

    Findings from the reports

    • The GSI consistently recorded high IRI values (often above 100 in/mi) on fresh concrete sections with dowel-bar ripple, reflecting severe roughness that persisted despite subsequent finishing efforts [1] [4].
    • The GSI’s IRI readings showed poor correlation with post-cure inertial profiler measurements because the device initially contained an extraneous three-foot wavelength component that artificially inflated roughness indices [1] [4].
    • The GSI reliably identified surface defects such as dowel-basket ripple and localized roughness in real-time, serving as an effective warning tool for contractors during paving operations [1] [4].
    • The device revealed a consistent pattern where the right side of the paver recorded higher IRI values than the left, which was attributed to stringless robotic total-station spacing issues [4].
    • Contractors used GSI data to adjust operational parameters, such as reducing total-station spacing, which resulted in smoother pavement outcomes and increased interest in purchasing the system for future work [4].

    Novelty & rigor — The approach is novel because it transforms the GOMACO Smoothness Indicator into a live decision-making tool that provides real-time feedback during paving, allowing contractors to immediately identify roughness sources and adjust machine settings before the concrete hardens [4]. This method introduces an innovative multi-stage sensor layout that captures continuous profile data at various points along the paving train, enabling the isolation of specific equipment or operations responsible for roughness in plastic concrete rather than relying solely on post-construction measurements [1]. The methodology is reproducible through a defined hardware setup involving specific sensor placements on slip-form pavers and trailing vehicles, combined with standardized operational procedures such as maintaining steady paving speeds and using manufacturer-specified mounting heights to ensure consistent data collection [1].

    Across the reviewed reports, the GSI consistently detected severe surface defects such as dowel-bar ripple and localized roughness in real-time, with IRI readings often exceeding 100 in/mi during active paving operations. However, these real-time measurements frequently diverged from post-construction inertial profiler values due to extraneous factors like sensor vibration and spurious wavelength content, limiting the device’s ability to directly predict final smoothness scores. Despite these correlation issues, the system proved effective as a warning tool for identifying operational variables affecting roughness, such as paver sensor spacing and finishing techniques, which contractors could then adjust to improve pavement quality. [1] [4]

    Implications — Practitioners should utilize the GSI as a real-time quality-control tool to identify and correct surface defects during construction, rather than relying on it for final smoothness scoring [1]. Users must account for the device’s inability to directly predict final IRI values and ensure that software updates addressing wavelength noise are applied to maintain data reliability [1].

    Limitations — The indicator cannot reliably predict final in-place smoothness values, making it unsuitable for directly meeting acceptance targets [1]. Raw measurements may contain extraneous wavelength components that require software correction to ensure data validity [1].
