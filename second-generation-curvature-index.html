    Second-Generation Curvature Index (2GCI)

    algorithmcurling-warpingpavement-analysisprofile-fitting
    Also known as: 2GCI
    Created 2026-07-22 · Updated 2026-08-16T12:25:16Z

    The Second-Generation Curvature Index (2GCI) is a pavement profiling methodology that isolates the geometric deformation of concrete slabs by fitting measured surface elevation data to a second-order polynomial model [1] [2]. This mathematical approach calculates a pseudo-strain gradient derived from theoretical slab mechanics to quantify the overall curvature resulting from temperature and moisture variations [1]. The technique yields specific deformation parameters, such as the radius of relative stiffness and maximum deflection ratios, which characterize the magnitude and direction of slab curling or warping independent of underlying grade irregularities [1] [2]. By linking these geometric properties to physical pavement characteristics like elastic modulus and subgrade support, the index provides a standardized metric for assessing curvature-induced roughness distinct from other distress mechanisms [1] [2].

    Second-Generation Curvature Index Algorithm

    The Second-Generation Curvature Index is a profiling technique that isolates the curling or warping shape of concrete slabs by fitting elevation data to a second-order polynomial after removing grade [2]. This mathematical approach quantifies the strain gradient required to transform an ideal flat slab into its observed warped state, incorporating both temperature-induced and moisture-induced effects [1]. By linking these curvature profiles to physical pavement properties such as geometry and subgrade support, the index provides a standardized method for assessing deformation separate from other distress mechanisms [1] [2].

    Research problem — There is a critical lack of reliable, standardized techniques to isolate and quantify the specific influence of concrete pavement curling and warping on ride quality [1]. Existing curvature indices suffer from insufficient accuracy, stability over time, and portability, which prevents engineers from effectively separating curvature-related roughness from other distress sources [1]. The absence of a robust field metric for extracting temperature- or moisture-induced deformation metrics hinders accurate performance prediction and maintenance planning, as these deformations can significantly alter overall roughness indices [2].

    State of the art — The Second-Generation Curvature Index (2GCI) is the leading quantitative tool for assessing concrete pavement curling and warping, offering greater accuracy and stability than earlier indices by linking measured slab profiles to physical parameters such as elastic modulus and subgrade support [1]. Modern practice embeds 2GCI in high-speed inertial profiler workflows, where it is routinely paired with standard profiling equipment to derive curvature-related metrics like Curvature IRI and normalized slab-deflection ratios for evaluating seasonal variations [1] [2]. Recent advances include the use of a complementary second-order polynomial model as an alternative yet comparably precise method for extracting curling and warping indicators from large field datasets [1].

    Findings from the reports

    • Developed a dedicated algorithm that fits raw high-speed profiler data to the Second-Generation Curvature Index (2GCI) model, generating curvature-related outputs such as curvature IRI, deflection, and deflection ratio for each concrete slab [1].
    • Validated the 2GCI-based results against a walking profiler and a portable curling/warping device, showing that the derived deflection and curvature values matched independent measurements and confirming the algorithm’s accuracy [1].
    • Observed that the 2GCI-derived curvature IRI, deflection, and deflection ratio all declined toward zero as the calculated total equivalent temperature difference approached 0 °F, indicating that permanent curling/warping effects vanish at that point [1].
    • Demonstrated that the three 2GCI indicators trend to zero as the total equivalent temperature difference approaches zero, confirming that the -10 °F default permanent curling/warping temperature used in AASHTOWare PMED is appropriate for Iowa pavements [1].
    • Showed that the 2GCI algorithm reliably extracts curling-and-warping indicators from high-speed profiler data by fitting a Westergaard-based curve whose pseudo-strain gradient captures temperature and moisture gradients [1].

    Novelty & rigor — The algorithm introduces an automated, end-to-end pipeline that integrates joint detection and grade removal with a Westergaard-based model fit to simultaneously solve for pseudo-strain gradient and radius of relative stiffness [1]. It uniquely isolates environmental deformation by generating a distinct Curvature IRI and a deflection ratio that minimizes joint-spacing bias, separating curling effects from general roughness metrics [1] [2]. The approach is reproducible through standardized MATLAB scripts that process raw inertial profiler data into continuous, grade-corrected profiles using nonlinear least-squares fitting with minimal user input [1] [2].
